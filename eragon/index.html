WHAT THE RESEARCH CHANGED

03

WHERE I WOULD START

Three bets.
A reason to change each.

Start with a small paid evaluation. The goal is a buying decision and a useful deployment, with the assumptions exposed.

THE OPPORTUNITY I WOULD DEFER

A broad regulated-enterprise rollout.

Control over infrastructure could matter deeply to a bank. I would first establish an approved workflow, an accountable sponsor, and the full cost of operating inside its boundary. ClearBank, Starling, and OakNorth stay on the research list; they are not my first deployment cohort.

WHAT I WOULD TEST FIRST

The first 90 days
should narrow the thesis.

Commercial progress includes delivery. Track the time and effort between a signed evaluation and accepted work.

  1. 01—30

    Find the repeated pain.

    Review existing deployments. Interview the four first-priority prospects or suitable peers. Establish a baseline, buyer, access path, and competing alternative.

    OUTPUT · TWO TESTABLE WORKFLOW HYPOTHESES
  2. 31—60

    Earn a production decision.

    Run a bounded paid evaluation. Replay held-out cases, launch with appropriate review, and measure total cost and accepted outcomes.

    OUTPUT · A DOCUMENTED BUY / STOP DECISION
  3. 61—90

    Repeat with less effort.

    Deploy the same pattern in a second account. Separate reusable skills and evaluations from customer configuration. Expand only where economics hold.

    OUTPUT · A NARROWER OFFER & REPEATABLE DEPLOYMENT

What I’d want to learn from the team.

  1. Which entry point converts and expands best today: Flux or workflow agents?
  2. What actually slows deployment: access, integrations, definitions, evaluation, or adoption?
  3. Which second deployment has become materially easier than the first?
  4. Where does Eragon consistently win against a customer’s existing tools or internal build?

How to read this work.

Researched 16 November 2026. The four anchors are customer relationships reported by Eragon. All 26 prospective accounts are independent hypotheses based on public product information. No prospect has been contacted for this project.

“Start”, “Explore”, and “Defer” are my commercial priorities. Confidence reflects the strength of the adjacency, not an estimate of purchase probability. Medium means relevant public evidence with an unvalidated need; Low means the connection is more speculative or the buying path is especially uncertain. None is sales-qualified.

Buying triggers are unknown unless evidenced. Company claims are distinguished from external research estimates. No source establishes Eragon revenue, account spend, or ROI. The value model uses invented assumptions.

Visual reference: Eragon’s fine-rule figure captions, architectural diagrams, and editorial typography. This independent atlas uses its own palette, layouts, and illustrations.
